Data, Methodology & Legal Notices

Open data feeds, licensing, and the rules of the road for this site

JCStream is generated exclusively from public records maintained by the Hamilton County Sheriff's Office (HCSO) and published through HCSO's public inmate-search portal at hcso.org. Everything the site is built from is published here as plain JSON, rebuilt by GitHub Actions on a best-effort schedule (cron every 15 minutes). That cadence is not a guarantee: observed refresh gaps have reached several hours during incidents, and each supplemental feed's row below states its own refresh-attempt cadence, not a freshness guarantee. We mirror the current roster: when HCSO removes a person, the next sweep removes them here too. There is no historical archive of released individuals.

anon_changelog.jsonPhase 11. PII-stripped append-only log of all change events, kept forever. The point: long-term institutional patterns (Friday vs Tuesday booking rates, felony share of roster over years) without keeping individual records visible after release. Access interruptions

JCStream retrieves the roster from HCSO’s public inmate-search portal. A requester’s right to inspect and copy public records from the public office is governed by R.C. 149.43, which imposes duties on the public office or person responsible for public records. Beginning 2026-05-30, that portal began returning blocked responses (HTTP 403, and at times HTTP 200 pages stripped of their results) to this site’s automated retrieval. Roster-list retrieval has since resumed; the roster list was updated 2026-09-25. Separately, inmate detail retrieval has been blocked since 2026-09-25; charges, bonds, and photos reflect last-good data, not the current roster.

Every blocked cycle, and each recovery, is recorded with a UTC timestamp in waf_block_log.json (hash-chained, and mirrored in this repository’s authenticated git history). JCStream does not attempt to circumvent the block: it preserves the last-known-good roster, declines to publish a partial one, and documents the interruption. The site does not route around HCSO’s controls.

Photos

Booking photos live at /photos/<inmate_number>.jpg - the same images HCSO embeds (base64) on its own inmate-detail page, re-encoded to 250×312 JPEG. A photo is present only if HCSO published one. They are removed when the inmate leaves the public roster.

How it’s built

A GitHub Actions cron sweeps hcso.org’s inmate search (substring match on last name - the 26 single letters cover the whole roster with dedup), parses each detail page, downscales the photo, diffs against the prior snapshot, pulls the Cincinnati Open Data feeds, regenerates this site, and commits. Source: github.com/AICincy/HCJC.

Crowdsourced courtclerk.org data

JCStream does not scrape courtclerk.org. The Hamilton County Clerk of Courts explicitly disallows automated access to /data/ in their robots.txt, and the prior maintainer of this project made the principled call to respect that limit. A scraper here would also break every time the Clerk's site changes.

But there's a path that works: any reader who looks up a case at courtclerk.org (passing their bot check in a regular browser) can paste the docket fields into a one-minute GitHub form. The form's a structured issue template; a workflow ingests it into data/courtclerk_cases.json; the next site build then displays the submission on the matching inmate's detail page under "Submitted by readers." Anyone reading that inmate's page sees the court information without anyone scraping anyone.

This is a deliberate trade: coverage will always be patchy, because only what someone bothers to submit appears. The upside: every entry was looked up by a real person, so the data quality on what does come in is higher than any scraper would manage.

Anonymized event log (permanent)

The roster mirror only keeps individuals while they're on the public roster: when HCSO releases someone, the next sweep removes them here. That's the privacy default, and it stays.

But aggregate counts shouldn't disappear too. anon_changelog.json is a parallel feed that retains every booking and release event forever, with all identifying fields (name, inmate_number, booking_number) stripped after 7 days. What survives the strip is the event type, the date (day-precision, not minute), the offense tier (F1-MM), and the primary charge category. That's enough to answer institutional-pattern questions ("how does Friday booking volume compare to Tuesday over the last year?", "what was the F1 share of the roster during summer 2025?") without keeping anyone individually visible after they've left custody.

Citing this data

There is no historical archive here: the roster mirror reflects only the current HCSO snapshot, so a citation should capture the snapshot it used (the generated_utc field, or the SHA256SUMS line for the file). Suggested format:

JCStream, Hamilton County Justice Center roster mirror, snapshot 2026-09-25T14:49:51Z, https://www.aretheyinjail.com/data/. Underlying records: Hamilton County Sheriff's Office public inmate roster (R.C. 149.43). Original selection, coordination, or arrangement by JCStream, to the extent copyrightable, licensed CC BY-NC 4.0.

For journalism and research use, note the caveats above: snapshot-in-time data, best-effort refresh cadence, no archive of released individuals, and the FCRA prohibition in the legal notices below.

Record removal & sealing compliance

When HCSO removes a record from its public inmate roster, this site removes the corresponding record on its next automated update cycle, typically within 30 minutes. This site does not maintain a historical archive of removed records. For records subject to a court order of sealing or expungement under ORC § 2953.32 (as amended - including changes by 135th G.A. HB 234 and 136th G.A. HB 96), this site will remove the record upon receiving notice of the order. To submit notice, or to report any error, open an issue at github.com/AICincy/HCJC/issues. Removal and correction requests are processed at no cost - there is never a fee, and there never will be. This site’s voluntary compliance with sealing and expungement orders reflects its respect for the rehabilitative purpose of Ohio’s sealing and expungement statutes (ORC §§ 2953.31-2953.61).
