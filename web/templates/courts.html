Which court is mine

Felony (prison time possible) → Court of Common Pleas, General Division

Misdemeanor or traffic → Hamilton County Municipal Court

Divorce, dissolution, or custody between parents → Domestic Relations Division

Under 18 → Juvenile Court

Estates, guardianship, marriage, adoption → Probate Court

Appealing a decision → First District Court of Appeals

The courts

Court of Common Pleas, General Division

The trial court of general jurisdiction: felony criminal cases and civil cases in which the amount in controversy is more than $15,000, including matters involving title to real estate. {{ common_pleas | length }} judges; cases are assigned by lot to a single judge at filing and stay with that judge through termination.

Mission. As an equal and independent branch of government, the Judges of the Hamilton County Court of Common Pleas, acting with integrity, shall provide a safe, accessible, transparent, efficient, and impartial system of justice by fairly resolving disputes, respecting the public interest, and preserving individual rights while maintaining the rule of law.

Pledge of Fairness. The fundamental mission of the Hamilton County Court of Common Pleas is to provide a fair and impartial forum for the resolution of disputes according to the rule of law. The Judges and staff therefore make this pledge to each litigant, defendant, victim, witness, juror, and person involved in a court proceeding: fairness includes the opportunity to be heard, the chance to have the court process explained, and the right to be treated with respect.

First District Court of Appeals

The intermediate appellate court for Hamilton County, the only county in the First District. Reviews appeals from Common Pleas (all divisions) and Municipal Court. Three-judge panels hear each case; panel decisions can be appealed to the Ohio Supreme Court.

Address
William Howard Taft Law Center, 12th Floor, Cincinnati, Ohio 45202
Opinion release
Wednesdays and Fridays.
Constitutional basis
Established by Article IV, Section 1 of the Ohio Constitution; jurisdiction outlined in Article IV, Section 3. Original jurisdiction over writs of habeas corpus, mandamus, procedendo, prohibition, and quo warranto.

How a case moves through the system

An adult felony arrest in Hamilton County typically follows this path. The path for a misdemeanor differs at step 2 onward (it stays in Municipal Court).

  1. Arrest by HCSO, Cincinnati PD, or another local jurisdiction. Booking at the Justice Center, 1000 Sycamore Street.
  2. Arraignment in Hamilton County Municipal Court (Room 205) within a short time after booking. The judge sets bond and reads the charges. For misdemeanors and traffic, the case stays in Municipal Court; for felonies, this is a preliminary hearing. See the Bond schedule page for the published starting-point amounts.
  3. If a felony, the case is bound over to the Court of Common Pleas, General Division (Room 410) after a preliminary hearing in Municipal Court or after a grand jury indictment.
  4. Case is assigned by lot to one Common Pleas judge under the Individual Assignment System (Local Rule 7). That judge handles every motion, hearing, plea, and trial in the case.
  5. Within 90 days of filing (120 days for foreclosure cases), the assigned judge holds a Case Management Conference to schedule the rest of the case (Local Rule 15).
  6. Resolution: plea, trial verdict, dismissal, or transfer to a specialty court (drug court, mental health court, Veterans Treatment Court).
  7. Sentence: probation, fines, restitution, county jail (typically misdemeanor), state prison (felonies). For diversion or treatment court, supervised release with treatment conditions.
  8. Appeal: from Common Pleas or Municipal Court, to the First District Court of Appeals (12th floor, William Howard Taft Law Center). From there, by leave, to the Ohio Supreme Court.

Specialty courts and probation supervision are covered in full on the Services and Programs tab; they are not duplicated here.

Visiting the courthouse

The courthouse is at 1000 Main Street, Cincinnati, Ohio 45202. Anyone entering must pass through a Sheriff's Security Checkpoint. Between 8:30 AM and 9:30 AM, expect much longer lines and wait times. The courthouse opens daily at 8:00 AM. Enter the courthouse from Main Street.
