Standard Bond Schedule

Hamilton County Courts · rev. 3/5/2026

The court's published starting point for misdemeanor and traffic bonds: money posted to secure release until the case is resolved. The judge sets the actual bond. This schedule covers Municipal Court misdemeanors and traffic offenses only. Felonies have no standard bond.

Understanding the schedule

1. What "10%" means

An entry like "$1,000 @ 10%" means the bond is set at $1,000 and the person posting pays 10 percent of that amount to secure release. Two examples from the schedule: $1,000 @ 10% means $100 posted; $2,500 @ 10% means $250 posted.

The three amount columns are residency tiers: in-county (lowest), out-of-county, and out-of-state or no permanent address (highest). The tier that applies depends on the arrestee's residence and whether they have a verifiable local address.

The schedule lists cash-percentage amounts and says nothing about surety bonds. Do not read the "10%" figure as a commercial bondsman's fee; the 10% is the share of the bond amount posted to the court.

Source: the court's Standard Bond Schedule, rev. 3/5/2026.

2. What "+ REVIEW" means

Several offenses, for example negligent homicide, vehicular homicide, vehicular assault, unlawful sexual contact with a minor, sexual imposition, voyeurism, and public indecency, show amounts like "$5,000 + REVIEW." The schedule itself does not define REVIEW, and the court's published materials used for this page do not describe the review's timing, location, or procedure.

Source: the court's Standard Bond Schedule, rev. 3/5/2026.

3. How, where, and when to post bond

  • Where. Bonds are posted with the Hamilton County Clerk of Courts at the Hamilton County Justice Center: the Clerk of Courts Municipal Criminal/Traffic Division, 1st Floor, 1000 Sycamore Street, Room 112. All bonds must be posted in Hamilton County.
  • Hours. 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Bond refund check pickup is narrower: Room 112, Monday through Friday, 8 am to 8 pm.
  • Payment. Cash, or credit/debit card: Mastercard, Visa, American Express, Discover. Photo ID is required to post bond. The Clerk's bond line is (513) 946-6010.

Source: the courts' scam alert (updated 06/09/2026) and the Clerk of Courts bond FAQ.

4. Bond refunds

The refund is generally 90% of the bond-amount portion. The state fee is refunded only if the case is dismissed, ignored by the grand jury, or ends in not guilty. Refunds are issued by check only, never credited back to a credit card, and are payable to the person who posted. A refund is released only to the surety unless a bond-authorization form names a third party, and it is issued within 14 days of the final disposition of all cases on the receipt.

Source: the Clerk of Courts bond FAQ.

5. Real-estate bonds

A defendant or surety may post Ohio real estate as bond. The property must be titled in the defendant's or the surety's name and located in Ohio (R.C. 2937.24). Property owned by a trust, corporation, LLC, partnership, or similar entity is not accepted, and property in foreclosure is not accepted. The surety must hold equity of at least twice the bond amount, over and above all encumbrances (R.C. 2937.281). A Surety Affidavit must be filed with the signatures of all current owners and spouses, all lien holders and balances with documentation, the county auditor's current valuation, the equity computation, and a certified copy of the deed from the county recorder. The Clerk may deny the property as bond if the terms are not met; knowingly false information is contempt and referral for prosecution.

Full requirements: Posting Real Estate for a Criminal Bond, Clerk of Courts memo (PDF).

Source: the Clerk of Courts memo "Posting Real Estate for a Criminal Bond."

6. Victim notification and protection

If you are a victim of the offense, you can get custody-status notifications: Ohio VINE, 1-800-770-0192, or register online at VINELink.

If you need protection from someone, Ohio courts can issue a protection order. Free help resources can connect you with advocates and the right court.

Source: public search 2026-09-22 (Ohio VINE / VINELink). The bond schedule itself is a posting-amount document; it says nothing about victim services.

About this page

Methodology

This page mirrors the court's Standard Bond Schedule (rev. 3/5/2026) as a searchable table. Amounts are shown exactly as published, including the "@ 10%", "O.R.", "NO BOND", and "+ REVIEW" flags, and the source PDF's "SEXAUL IMPOSITION" [sic] spelling (ORC 2907-06) is preserved verbatim, not silently corrected. The site does not compute the amount you would pay.
